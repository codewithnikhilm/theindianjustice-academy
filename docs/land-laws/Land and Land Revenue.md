# Chapter VII: Land and Land Revenue
*The Karnataka Land Revenue Act, 1964 (Sections 67 to 90)*

---

## Overview

Chapter VII lies at the core of the Karnataka Land Revenue Act, 1964. It defines the State’s underlying ownership rights over natural resources and public properties, establishes the universal liability of landholders to pay land revenue, and sets down the statutory mechanisms for land disposal, management, and revenue recovery.

> 💡 **Analogy:** > The relationship between the State and landholders under this Act is much like a **Master Landlord and a Perpetual Leaseholder**. While the leaseholder (the occupant) has the right to use and transfer the land, the Master Landlord (the State) retains the underlying rights to the most valuable "treasures" (minerals, water systems, and reserved flora) and requires a mandatory "subscription fee" (land revenue) to maintain the system—a fee that takes priority over all other personal or mortgage debts.

---

## 1. Ownership and State Sovereignty over Land (Sections 67 to 90)

The State Government is declared the ultimate owner of all lands and natural features that are not the explicit property of private individuals or specific groups:

* **Public Infrastructure:** All public roads, streets, lanes, bridges, ditches, dykes, and fences.
* **Natural Water Bodies:** The bed of the sea, harbors, rivers, streams, lakes, tanks, canals, and all standing or flowing water.
* **Mines & Minerals:** Rights to all mines, minerals, and mineral products vest **absolutely in the State Government**, notwithstanding any prior grants, deeds, or laws.
* **Trees & Forests:** The State reserves statutory rights to specific timber/trees (especially roadside trees and those within reserved forests). Rights to trees on private holdings depend on whether a formal survey settlement has been finalized.

> **Dispute Resolution:** If a private party claims title or property rights against the State, the **Deputy Commissioner** (or a high-ranking Survey Officer) is empowered to hold a formal inquiry and issue a binding order deciding the claim.

---

## 2. The Nature & Principles of Land Revenue

**Land Revenue** is broadly defined as any sum received or claimable by the Government from any person on account of land held by or vested in them, including taxes, cesses, rates, or other legal imposts.

* **Universal Liability:** Every piece of land—whether utilized for agricultural or non-agricultural purposes—is liable to pay land revenue unless specifically exempted by a contract or official notification.
* **Paramount Charge:** Arrears of land revenue form a **paramount charge** on the land holding. The State's recovery claim takes legal precedence over all other mortgages, judgments, attachments, or private debts.
* **Basis of Assessment:** Assessment is primarily calculated based on the *agricultural use* of the land. Diverting land to non-agricultural uses (e.g., commercial, residential, or industrial) triggers distinct non-agricultural assessment rates or commutation fees.
* **Ordinary Land Revenue:** Specifically refers to the standard revenue payable when no special water advantages provided by the State Government are utilized.

---

## 3. Disposal and Management of Land

The Act authorizes multiple avenues for managing and disposing of State-owned lands:

* **Disposal for Public Good:** The State Government, Regional Commissioners, Deputy Commissioners, and Tahsildars may assign or grant lands for agriculture, industrial development, or public utilities under prescribed rules.
* **Public Auctions:** High-value lands (properties likely to fetch market values far above standard upset prices) are generally disposed of through transparent public auctions.
* **Special Assignments (Gomal / Forest Reserves):** The Deputy Commissioner may set aside specific lands for community needs, such as free cattle pasturage (*Gomal* land) or forest reserves. Lands so assigned **cannot be diverted** to other uses without official sanction.
* **Extinction of Public Rights:** If a public road, path, or street is no longer required, the Government may formally extinguish public rights over it, provided public objections are invited and due compensation is paid to affected parties.

## 4. Realisation of Revenue & Default Procedures

The **occupant** (for unalienated land) or the **superior holder** (for alienated land) is primarily responsible for paying land revenue on or before the due date.

### Consequences of Non-Payment:
1. **Defaulter Status:** Unpaid dues become "arrears of land revenue," and the party responsible is legally classified as a **defaulter**.
2. **Preventive Crop Detention:** The State has the power to prevent the harvesting or removal of crops from the land until the land revenue for the current year is fully secured or paid.
