The procedure for Appeal and Revision within the revenue administration is detailed in Chapter V of The Karnataka Land Revenue Act, 1964. This chapter establishes the hierarchy of appellate authorities, limitations on filing appeals, the powers of appellate authorities, and the authority for revision.

I. Appeals
The Act distinguishes between two types of appeals: appeals from original orders (First Appeal) and Second Appeals.

A. Appeals from Original Orders (First Appeals)
An appeal lies from every original order passed under the Act, the rules made thereunder, or any order made under Section 54 of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908. The appellate authority depends on the rank of the officer who passed the original order:
Officer Passing the Original Order	Appellate Authority (First Appeal)
Revenue Officer subordinate to the Assistant Commissioner (whether or not invested or delegated with the powers of the AC or DC)	Assistant Commissioner
Assistant Commissioner (whether or not invested with the powers of the DC)	Deputy Commissioner
Deputy Commissioner	The Tribunal
Regional Commissioner	The Tribunal
Survey Officer below the rank of an Assistant Director of Land Records or Assistant Director for Settlement	Assistant Director of Land Records or Assistant Director for Settlement
Survey Officer of the rank of an Assistant Director of Land Records or Assistant Director for Settlement	Joint Director of Land Records or Joint Director for Settlement
Joint Director of Land Records or Joint Director for Settlement	Director of Survey, Settlement and Land Records
Director of Survey, Settlement and Land Records	The Tribunal
B. Second Appeals
A second appeal lies against any order passed in a first appeal under Section 49. The hierarchy for second appeals is as follows:
• If the first appeal order was passed by the Assistant Commissioner, the second appeal lies to the Deputy Commissioner.
• If the first appeal order was passed by the Deputy Commissioner, the second appeal lies to the Tribunal.
• If the first appeal order was passed by the Assistant Director for Settlement or Assistant Director of Land Records, the second appeal lies to the Director of Survey, Settlement and Land Records.
• If the first appeal order was passed by the Joint Director of Land Records or Joint Director for Settlement, or by the Director of Survey, Settlement and Land Records, the second appeal lies to the Tribunal.
Finality: An order passed on second appeal shall be final.

C. Appellate Procedure and Powers
1. Limitation of Appeals (Time Limits):
    ◦ First Appeal: Must be filed after the expiry of sixty days from the date of the order appealed against.
    ◦ Second Appeal: Must be filed after the expiry of ninety days from the date of the order appealed against.
    ◦ The provisions of Sections 4, 5, and 12 of the Limitation Act, 1963, apply mutatis mutandis (with necessary changes) to all appeals under the Act.
2. Documents: Every petition of appeal must be accompanied by a certified copy of the order appealed from, unless the appellate authority waives this requirement.
3. Powers of Appellate Authority: The appellate authority may, for reasons recorded in writing, choose to:
    ◦ Annul, reverse, modify, or confirm the order appealed from.
    ◦ Direct the officer who made the order (or a subordinate officer) to conduct further inquiry or take additional evidence on specified points.
    ◦ The appellate authority may also conduct such inquiry or take additional evidence itself.
4. Restrictions on Additional Evidence: Additional evidence (oral or documentary) shall generally not be directed to be taken, unless:
    ◦ The Revenue Officer whose order is appealed from refused to admit evidence that should have been admitted.
    ◦ The appellate authority requires a document or witness to be examined to enable it to pronounce orders.
    ◦ The appellate authority allows such evidence or document/witness examination for any substantial cause.
    ◦ If additional evidence is allowed, the appellate authority must record the reason for its admission.
5. Stay of Execution of Orders:
    ◦ The Revenue Officer who passed the order (or their successor) may direct the execution of the order to be stayed before the expiry of the appeal period, for the time necessary to file the appeal and obtain a stay order from the appellate authority.
    ◦ The appellate authority may direct that the execution of the appealed order be stayed for such time as it thinks fit, or until the decision of the appeal, whichever is earlier. This authority may also cancel or vary a stay order if sufficient cause is shown.
    ◦ No stay order may be passed except in accordance with these provisions.
6. Orders and Irregularities: No order passed by a Revenue Officer shall be reversed or altered in appeal or revision due to an error, omission, or irregularity in the process (summons, notice, proclamation, warrant, order, or other proceedings), unless such error, omission, or irregularity has, in fact, occasioned a failure of justice.


II. Revision
The power of revision allows superior officers and the Tribunal to examine the records of subordinate proceedings.

A. Authorities Empowered to Exercise Revision
The following authorities may call for and examine the record of any inquiry or proceedings of any subordinate officer under the Act or under Section 54 of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, to satisfy themselves as to the legality or propriety of the proceedings:
• The Tribunal.
• Any Revenue Officer not inferior in rank to an Assistant Commissioner.
• Any Survey Officer not inferior in rank to a Deputy Director of Land Records or an Assistant Settlement Officer in their respective departments.

B. Subordination for Revision Purposes
For the purposes of the revision power under Section 56(1):
• A Special Deputy Commissioner is deemed to be not subordinate to the Deputy Commissioner.
• All Revenue Officers are deemed to be subordinate to the Tribunal.

C. Powers in Revision
If, upon examining the record, it appears to the Tribunal or the empowered officer that any decision, order, or proceeding should be modified, annulled, or reversed, the authority may pass such order as deemed fit.
Condition: No order shall be modified, annulled, or reversed unless notice has been served on the parties interested and an opportunity given to them of being heard.

D. Limitation for Revision
• No application for revision shall be entertained unless it is presented within a period of four months from the date of the order.
• No power of revision on application shall be exercised against any order in respect of which an appeal under Chapter V has been preferred.
• However, a Revenue Officer or Survey Officer referred to in Section 56(1) may exercise suo motu (on his own motion) power of revision in respect of any order against which no appeal has been preferred under Chapter V, at any time within three years from the date of the order sought to be revised.
• When calculating the period of limitation, any period during which the revision proceeding is stayed by a court order or injunction shall be excluded.

E. Final Orders and the Tribunal
When the Act declares that an order of a Revenue Officer shall be final, this means that no appeal lies from such order. However, the Tribunal alone is competent to modify, annul, or reverse any such expressly final order using its powers of revision under Section 56.

III. Transitional Appeals and Amendments
The statutory provisions regarding appeals have been subject to amendments to ensure clarity and centralise authority:
• Appeals from Repealed Acts: Regarding orders or proceedings commenced and concluded under Acts repealed by the 1964 Act, the appellate functions previously handled by the Divisional Commissioners were entrusted to the Mysore Revenue Appellate Tribunal (KRAT).
• Clarification of Tahsildar Appeals: An amendment was made to Section 49 to clarify that an appeal against an order passed by a Revenue Officer subordinate to the Assistant Commissioner, even if delegated powers of the Assistant Commissioner or Deputy Commissioner, still lies to the Assistant Commissioner.
• Appeals in Land Grant Cases: Appeals in land grant cases were initially vested in the KRAT. They were temporarily shifted to the Divisional Commissioners, with revision vesting in the Government. Subsequently, due to high pendency, the appellate and revisional powers for land grant cases were transferred back to the Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal (KRAT) in 1975, making it the highest appellate authority in revenue matters in general.
• Abolition of Divisional Commissioners: Upon the proposed abolition of Divisional Commissioners, their appellate and revisional powers were proposed to be entrusted to the Tribunal.
