# Chapter III: Procedure of Revenue Officers and Revenue Courts

*The Karnataka Land Revenue Act, 1964 (Sections 24 to 39)*

---

## Part I: Status and Scope of Revenue Courts

* **Revenue Court Status:** Any Revenue Officer not below the rank of a **Tahsildar**—when exercising power under the Act (or any other law in force) to inquire into or decide a question between the State Government and any person, or between parties to any proceedings—is deemed to be a **Revenue Court**.
* **Inherent Powers:** Revenue Courts possess the inherent power to make necessary orders to ensure the ends of justice or to prevent the abuse of the court's process.

---

## Part II: Inquiry and Hearing Procedures

### 1. Place for Holding Enquiries

* **Jurisdiction Limit:** A Revenue Officer cannot hear or inquire into any case outside the local limits of their jurisdiction unless reasons are recorded in writing and approved by a superior officer.
* **Headquarters Exception:** If an officer’s official headquarters lies outside their local jurisdiction, they may hold inquiries/hearings at that location without requiring special direction or approval.

---

### 2. Power to Summon and Take Evidence

Officers **not lower in rank than a Tahsildar or an Assistant Director of Land Records** have the power to administer oaths, summon parties or witnesses, and require document production.

* **Compulsory Attendance:** Summoned persons must appear either in person or via an authorized agent. Exemptions under Sections 132 and 133 of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, apply.
* **Obligations:** Witnesses and parties must tell the truth and produce requested documents.
* **Document Production Only:** A person summoned strictly to produce a document may cause the document to be delivered rather than attending in person.
* **Examination on Commission:** If a person cannot attend due to sickness, infirmity, or legal exemption, the officer may issue a commission to a subordinate officer to record their evidence.

> **Penalties for Non-Compliance:** > If a summoned person fails to appear, the officer may:
> 1. Issue a **bailable warrant of arrest**.
> 2. Order the person to furnish **security for appearance**.
> 3. Impose a fine not exceeding **₹20**.

* **Summons Requirements:** Must be in writing (in duplicate), state the purpose, time, and place of appearance, and bear the signature and seal of the issuing officer.
* **Service Methods:**
* **Personal:** Delivered directly to the person or an adult male family member residing with them.
* **Substituted:** If no adult male member is available, a copy is affixed to a conspicuous part of the house. If the person resides in another district, it is sent to that district's Deputy Commissioner for service.
* **Registered Post:** May be served via prepaid registered post. The postal acknowledgment signature serves as *prima facie* proof of sufficient service.


* **Notices:** Served by delivering a copy to the person/agent or by affixing it to a conspicuous place on the land concerned. Minor errors in designation/names do not invalidate a notice unless substantial injustice occurs.

### 4. Types of Inquiries

| Parameter | Formal Inquiry (Sec. 33) | Summary Inquiry (Sec. 34) | Other Inquiries |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Record of Evidence** | Recorded in full in writing (Kannada, English, or prescribed language) and signed by the officer. | Brief summary of evidence and minute of proceedings recorded in the officer's own hand. | Conducted according to prescribed rules or the officer's discretion for public good. |
| **Orders / Decisions** | Contains a full statement of the grounds/reasons for the order. | Record includes material averments, the decision, and basic reasons. | Formulated as necessary to ascertain essential facts. |
| **Flexibility** | Strictly follows full statutory procedure. | The officer may choose to apply formal inquiry rules to a summary inquiry. | Determined by general or special orders. |

---

### 5. Judicial Status and Conduct of Hearings

* **Judicial Proceedings:** Both formal and summary inquiries are deemed judicial proceedings for the purposes of Sections 193, 219, and 228 of the Indian Penal Code. The inquiring officer operates as a **Civil Court**.
* **Public Hearing & Pronouncement:**
* Hearings must be open to the public with proper notice given to parties.
* Orders must be signed and pronounced in open court on a scheduled date. If a party is absent, the decision's substance must be communicated by post.


* **Ex-Parte Orders & Restoration:**
* If a party fails to appear after service, the case may be decided *ex-parte* or dismissed for default.
* **Setting Aside Default Orders:** An aggrieved party may apply within **30 days** (from the date of order, or date of knowledge if notice was not served) to set aside the default/ex-parte order upon showing sufficient cause.

## Part IV: Powers of Entry

A Revenue Officer, along with authorized servants and workmen under their control, may enter any land or premises for lawful official tasks, including:

* Land measurement and surveying.
* Fixing or inspecting boundaries.
* Soil classification and assessment.

> **Restriction on Entry into Dwellings:** > A minimum of **7 days' prior notice** must be given before entering any dwelling house or attached enclosed courtyard/garden without the occupier's consent. Officers must strictly respect the social and religious sentiments of the occupiers.
