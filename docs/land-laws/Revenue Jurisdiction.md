# Chapter VI: Revenue Jurisdiction
*The Karnataka Land Revenue Act, 1964 (Sections 60 to 66)*

---

## Overview

Chapter VI establishes a clear boundary between the authority of **Revenue Courts** and **Civil Courts**. It grants exclusive jurisdiction to Revenue Courts over land revenue administration while creating a strict jurisdictional bar for Civil Courts on specified matters.

> 💡 **Analogy:** > Revenue Jurisdiction acts like a specialized gatekeeper for a high-security estate. While the "Civil Court" gate is open for general disputes between neighbors, any dispute involving the "Estate's internal rules" (taxes, boundary markers, or the Landlord's official records) must go through the "Revenue Court" gate first. You aren't even allowed to knock on the Civil Court's door until you have finished talking to every official inside the estate's own hierarchy.

---

## 1. Scope & Definitions

In the specific context of revenue jurisdiction:

* **Land:** Broadly defined to include village sites, towns, cities, trees, crops, grass, and rights of way.
* **Land Revenue:** Includes all sums received or claimable by the State Government on account of land, including taxes, cesses, and rates.

---

## 2. Exclusive Jurisdiction & The Bar on Civil Courts

The Act explicitly bars Civil Courts from exercising jurisdiction over matters that Revenue Courts are specifically empowered to handle.



### Matters Excluded from Civil Courts:

* **Office & Service Claims:** Claims against the Government relating to property appertaining to any office or service.
* **Objections to Assessment:** Challenges to the amount, incidence, mode, or principle of land revenue assessment, or the validity of survey/settlement notifications.
* **Realisation of Revenue:** Claims arising out of proceedings for the recovery of land revenue or other public demands.
* **Settlement & Survey Records:** Claims against the Government to be entered in survey or settlement records as a holder, occupant, or tenant, or to have such entries omitted or amended.
* **Land Distribution & Partition:** Allotment of land revenue or the distribution of land on partition.
* **Exemptions & Waste Land:** Claims to hold land free from revenue or disputes regarding the occupation of vacant/waste land belonging to the Government.
* **Boundaries & Marks:** Claims regarding boundaries fixed under the Act or official orders regarding survey marks.

---

## 3. Savings: When Civil Courts Retain Jurisdiction

Under Sections 61 and 62, Civil Courts remain competent to hear specific types of actions:

1. **Legal Exemptions:** Claims for revenue exemption based on a specific statute or a written grant issued by the Government.
2. **Suits for Excess Payment:** Lawsuits contesting an amount paid under protest if it exceeds authorized limits or has already been satisfied.
3. **Private Rights & Title:** Disputes between private parties to establish private title, rights, or possession of land (e.g., survey numbers or sub-divisions), even if affected by land record entries.

---

## 4. Mandatory Exhaustion of Remedies

Before instituting any suit or legal proceeding in a Civil Court against the State Government or a Revenue Officer, a plaintiff **must exhaust all statutory rights of appeal**.

> **Procedural Rule:** The Civil Court cannot entertain the suit unless the plaintiff proves that all administrative appeals allowed under the law have been fully presented and exhausted.
