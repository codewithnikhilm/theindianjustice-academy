# Revenue Jurisdiction under the Karnataka Land Revenue Act, 1964
# Chapter VI
# Sections 60 to 66

The **Revenue Jurisdiction** under the Karnataka Land Revenue Act, 1964, is primarily governed by **Chapter VI (Sections 60 to 66)**. This Chapter establishes the boundary between the jurisdiction of **Revenue Courts and Civil Courts** in matters relating to land revenue and revenue administration.

# 1. Scope and Definitions

The Act provides definitions that are relevant to understanding the jurisdiction of Revenue Courts.

## 1.1 Land

For the purposes of the Act, **"land"** has a broad meaning and includes:

- Village sites;
- Towns;
- Cities;
- Trees;
- Crops;
- Grass; and
- Rights of way.

## 1.2 Land Revenue

**"Land revenue"** is also defined broadly and includes all sums received or claimable by the Government on account of land.

It includes:

- Taxes;
- Cesses;
- Rates; and
- Other amounts connected with land revenue.

# 2. Exclusive Jurisdiction of Revenue Courts

The Act gives Revenue Courts exclusive jurisdiction over matters which they are empowered to determine under the Karnataka Land Revenue Act.

Consequently, Civil Courts are barred from exercising jurisdiction over matters that fall within the exclusive jurisdiction of Revenue authorities.

## 2.1 Claims Relating to Government Property and Services

Civil Courts cannot entertain claims against the Government relating to property appertaining to an office or property connected with the performance of a service.

## 2.2 Objections to Assessment

Civil Courts cannot entertain objections concerning:

- The amount of land revenue assessment;
- The incidence of assessment;
- The mode of assessment;
- The principle of assessment; or
- The validity of survey or settlement notifications.

Such matters fall within the revenue jurisdiction.

## 2.3 Realisation of Land Revenue

Claims arising from proceedings for the recovery or realisation of:

- Land revenue; or
- Other public demands;

are generally outside the jurisdiction of Civil Courts.

## 2.4 Survey and Settlement Records

Civil Courts cannot ordinarily entertain claims against the Government seeking to:

- Have a person entered in survey or settlement records as a holder;
- Have a person entered as an occupant;
- Have a person entered as a tenant; or
- Have an existing entry omitted or amended.

Such matters are dealt with through the revenue machinery.

## 2.5 Allotment and Distribution of Land

Questions relating to:

- Allotment of land revenue; or
- Distribution of land upon partition;

fall within the revenue jurisdiction where the Act assigns such matters to Revenue authorities.

## 2.6 Exemption from Revenue and Occupation of Government Land

Civil Courts cannot ordinarily entertain claims relating to:

- Holding land free from payment of revenue; or
- Occupation of vacant or waste land belonging to the Government.

## 2.7 Boundaries and Survey Marks

Civil Courts are barred from entertaining claims concerning:

- Boundaries fixed under the Act; or
- Orders relating to boundaries and survey marks;

where the matter falls within the statutory jurisdiction of Revenue authorities.

# 3. Savings — Matters Where Civil Courts Retain Jurisdiction

The bar on Civil Court jurisdiction is not absolute.

Sections 61 and 62 preserve the jurisdiction of Civil Courts in certain circumstances.

## 3.1 Claims Based on Legal Exemption

Where a person claims exemption from payment of revenue on the basis of:

- A specific law; or
- A written grant from the Government;

the claim may be cognisable by a Civil Court.

## 3.2 Suits for Excess Payment

A suit may be brought to contest an amount paid under protest where:

- The amount paid was in excess of what was authorised; or
- The amount had already been satisfied.

Thus, the revenue jurisdiction does not completely exclude Civil Court jurisdiction over every dispute concerning payment.

## 3.3 Private Rights

Civil Courts retain jurisdiction over suits between private parties for establishing **private rights**.

This may include suits relating to:

- Establishment of private rights in land; or
- Possession of land, including a survey number or sub-division.

Such a suit may be maintainable even where the claimed private right is affected by an entry in the revenue records.

# 4. Mandatory Exhaustion of Remedies

An important procedural requirement is that a person must generally **exhaust the available statutory remedies of appeal** before approaching a Civil Court in a suit or proceeding against the State Government or a Revenue Officer.

The plaintiff must establish that all appeals permitted by law have been presented before the Civil Court can entertain the matter, where the statutory requirement applies.

This principle prevents parties from bypassing the revenue appellate hierarchy and directly approaching the Civil Court.

# 5. Interaction with the High Court

The Karnataka Land Revenue Act provides mechanisms for resolving questions concerning the jurisdiction of Revenue Courts and Civil Courts.

## 5.1 Reference by the Tribunal

If a question of law or jurisdiction arises during a proceeding before the Tribunal and the Tribunal considers that the question requires determination by the High Court, it may refer the question to the High Court.

## 5.2 Reference by Civil Court

A Civil Judge may face a question as to whether a particular suit is barred by the revenue jurisdiction provisions.

Where the Civil Judge is uncertain whether the Civil Court is precluded from entertaining the suit, the question of jurisdiction may be referred to the High Court.

## 5.3 High Court Composition

References concerning such questions of jurisdiction are required to be heard by a **Bench consisting of at least two Judges**.

# 6. Revenue Court and Civil Court — Basic Distinction

The distinction may be understood as follows:

| Revenue Court | Civil Court |
|---|---|
| Deals with matters specifically assigned to Revenue authorities by the Act | Deals primarily with civil disputes and private rights |
| Determines matters relating to revenue assessment and administration | Determines disputes concerning civil and private rights where jurisdiction is not barred |
| Deals with survey and settlement matters assigned to it by law | May determine private rights even where revenue records contain an entry affecting those rights |
| Exercises statutory appellate and revisional powers under revenue laws | Exercises ordinary civil jurisdiction subject to statutory bars |
| Its jurisdiction is created and limited by revenue legislation | Its jurisdiction is generally governed by civil procedural and substantive law |

# 7. Purpose of the Bar on Civil Court Jurisdiction

The bar on Civil Court jurisdiction serves an important administrative purpose.

Revenue authorities possess specialised knowledge concerning:

- Land revenue;
- Survey;
- Settlement;
- Boundaries;
- Land records;
- Government land; and
- Revenue administration.

Allowing every such dispute to be immediately brought before a Civil Court could result in duplication of proceedings and interference with the statutory revenue machinery.

The Act therefore creates a specialised system for matters entrusted to Revenue authorities while preserving Civil Court jurisdiction over specified private and civil rights.

# 8. Important Principle

The existence of a revenue record entry does not necessarily mean that every dispute concerning the underlying private right becomes a revenue matter.

Where the dispute concerns a **private right between private parties**, the Civil Court may retain jurisdiction where the statutory provisions permit it.

Therefore, it is important to distinguish between:

1. **Correction or determination of revenue records under the revenue law**, and
2. **Determination of substantive private rights**, such as title or possession, where Civil Court jurisdiction is preserved.

# 9. Analogy

The revenue jurisdiction can be understood as a **specialised gatekeeper** for the land administration system.

The **Revenue Court gate** deals with matters concerning the internal administration of land, such as:

- Land revenue;
- Assessment;
- Survey;
- Settlement;
- Boundaries; and
- Government revenue records.

The **Civil Court gate** remains available for general civil disputes and private rights where the law does not exclude its jurisdiction.

Therefore, a person cannot ordinarily bypass the statutory revenue machinery by directly approaching a Civil Court in a matter that the Karnataka Land Revenue Act has specifically entrusted to Revenue authorities.

# 10. Conclusion

The jurisdictional scheme under **Chapter VI of the Karnataka Land Revenue Act, 1964** creates a clear division between Revenue Courts and Civil Courts.

Revenue authorities have exclusive jurisdiction over matters specifically entrusted to them, particularly those concerning **land revenue, assessment, survey, settlement, boundaries and revenue records**.

At the same time, the Act preserves the jurisdiction of Civil Courts in specified circumstances, particularly where the dispute concerns **private rights or claims based on legally recognised exemptions**.

The provisions therefore attempt to maintain a balance between **specialised revenue adjudication** and the ordinary jurisdiction of Civil Courts.

The overall principle is that a dispute must be brought before the forum to which the legislature has assigned jurisdiction, and parties cannot ordinarily bypass the statutory revenue hierarchy where the Act expressly bars Civil Court jurisdiction.
