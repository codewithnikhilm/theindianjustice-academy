# Chapter XI: Record of Rights
*The Karnataka Land Revenue Act, 1964 (Sections 127 to 136)*

---

## Overview

Chapter XI details the statutory framework governing the **Record of Rights (RoR)** (commonly known as *Pahani* or RTC), the procedure for reporting and certifying **mutations**, the evidentiary value of land records, and the mechanisms for dispute resolution.

---

## 1. Preparation & Contents of the Record of Rights (RoR)

A Record of Rights must be prepared and maintained for every village in the prescribed manner.

### Key Contents:
* **Names of Interested Parties:** Holders, occupants, owners, mortgagees, landlords, tenants, or assignees of rent/revenue.
* **Extent of Interest:** The nature and scope of individual rights, conditions, and attached liabilities.
* **Financial Dues:** Rent or land revenue payable by or to any of the recorded persons.
* **Other Prescribed Particulars:** Crop details, soil classification, and encumbrances.

> **Gazette Notification:** Upon completion of the RoR for a village, the fact must be officially notified in the Government Gazette and published locally.

---

## 2. Reporting Acquisition of Rights (Mutations)

Whenever a right in land is acquired, it must be reported to the village officer to ensure land records remain up-to-date.

### Key Principles:
* **Obligation & Timeline:** Any person acquiring a right via succession, inheritance, partition, purchase, mortgage, gift, lease, or otherwise must report the acquisition **within 3 months** (orally or in writing).
* **Minors & Legal Guardians:** Guardians or property managers must report on behalf of minors or incapacitated individuals.
* **Exemption:** Rights acquired via **registered documents** are exempt from the manual reporting obligation (the registration office notifies the revenue department directly).
* **Partition Requirements:** Partition-based claims **must** include a boundary sketch prepared by a **Licensed Surveyor**.
* **Scope of Rights:** Includes mortgages without possession; excludes simple easements or charges under Section 100 of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882.

### Delayed Reporting Penalties:
* **3 Months to 1 Year:** Fixed penalty of **₹2**.
* **Beyond 1 Year:** Penalty between **₹2 and ₹10** (ordered by the Tahsildar or Revenue Officer).

---

## 3. The Mutation and Dispute Process

The procedure for updating the Record of Rights follows a strict administrative workflow:

1. **Register of Mutations:** Every reported right is recorded in the Register of Mutations.
2. **Public Notice:**
   * A copy of the entry is posted conspicuously in the village *Chavadi*.
   * Written notices are served to all persons appearing to have an interest in the land.
3. **Disputed Cases Register:** Any oral or written objection is transferred to the **Register of Disputed Cases**.
4. **Inquiry & Orders:** Objections are formally inquired into by a designated officer (exercising powers under Chapter III). Orders disposing of the dispute are entered in the Register of Mutations.
5. **Certification & Final Transfer:** Entries in the Register of Mutations must be tested and certified by a prescribed officer. **No entry is transferred to the official Record of Rights until it is certified.**

---

## 4. Documentation & Evidentiary Value

### Patta Book
* Issued to every agricultural landholder and primary tenant.
* Contains extracts of the RoR, details of revenue payments, government dues, and cultivation/crop statistics.
* Designed to help landholders secure agricultural credit and loans easily without administrative delays.

### Legal Presumption of Truth
> **Statutory Presumption:** Every certified entry in the Record of Rights, Register of Mutations, or Patta Book is **presumed to be true** until the contrary is proved or a new entry is lawfully substituted.

### Obligation to Provide Information
Landholders are legally bound to produce necessary documents or information requested by compiling officers within **30 days**. Failure to comply carries a penalty of up to **₹25**, recoverable as land revenue arrears.

---

## 5. Jurisdiction, Appeals & Civil Court Interaction

* **Exclusion of Civil Court Jurisdiction:** No civil lawsuit can be filed against the Government or officers seeking to compel or prevent entries in the RoR.
* **Declaratory Suits Permitted:** An aggrieved party in possession may file a suit for a **Declaration of Title** under the Specific Relief Act. Upon the court's decree, the revenue entries must be modified accordingly.
* **Appeals:**
  * Chapter V general appeal rules do **not** apply to Chapter XI.
  * An appeal against a dispute order or certified entry must be filed within **60 days** before the prescribed authority, whose decision is final.
* **Revision:** The **Deputy Commissioner** holds *suo motu* or application-based revisional powers over RoR and Mutation entries, provided affected parties are given a fair hearing.

### Mandatory Court Submissions:
* Plaintiffs in land disputes **must attach certified copies** of the RoR/Mutation entries with their court plaints.
* Civil Courts are legally required to notify the Deputy Commissioner of any final decrees altering land titles so that the public records can be formally updated.
