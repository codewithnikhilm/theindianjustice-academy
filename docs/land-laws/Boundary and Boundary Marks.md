# Chapter XII: Boundaries and Boundary Marks

## The Karnataka Land Revenue Act, 1964 (Sections 137–147)

## Overview

Chapter XII governs the determination, construction, maintenance, and protection of boundaries and boundary marks. These physical indicators define land holdings, establish legal rights, and provide certainty for land administration across Karnataka.

> 💡 **Analogy:** > Boundary marks act as the "silent sentinels" of property law; though they are often simple stones or ridges, they carry the full weight of the state's authority, serving as a permanent physical record of a legal agreement that even a Civil Court cannot easily overturn.

---

## 1. Definitions and Legal Status

* **Boundary Mark:** Any erection (earth, stone, or other material), hedge, unploughed ridge, strip of ground, or other natural/artificial object set up or specified by a Survey or Revenue Officer to determine the boundary of any division of land.
* **Survey Mark:** Any mark or object erected or specified by a Survey Officer to indicate the position or level of any specific geographic point.
* **Finality of Settlement:** The official settlement of a boundary is conclusive. It permanently determines the correct position of the boundary line and the legal rights of landholders on either side regarding the land in question.

---

## 2. Determination of Boundaries

The Act lays down systematic procedures for establishing boundaries at different levels:

### A. Village Boundaries
* **By Agreement:** If adjoining Gram Panchayats agree on a boundary line, the designated officer records the agreement, marks the boundary, and it is deemed officially settled.
* **By Inquiry (Disputed):** If Panchayats disagree or a dispute is pending, the officer must conduct a survey, prepare a plan of the disputed land, and decide the boundary via a formal inquiry. Appeals lie to the **Joint Director of Land Records**, whose decision is final.

### B. Survey Numbers and Holdings
* **Undisputed Holdings:** Recorded and marked as pointed out by the holder or occupant.
* **Disputed Holdings:** Fixed by a Survey Officer based on official land records and necessary inquiries.
* **Post-Survey Disputes:** If a boundary dispute arises after a survey settlement is already completed, the **Tahsildar** hears and decides the matter based on land records or a fresh inquiry.

### C. Arbitration
Parties to a boundary dispute may voluntarily nominate a **sole arbitrator** or a **three-person arbitration committee**. Their decision, once confirmed by the Joint Director of Land Records, becomes binding and final.

---

## 3. Construction and Maintenance



The physical maintenance and upkeep of boundary marks is a shared legal responsibility:

* **Duty of Landholders:** Every landholder is legally responsible for maintaining and keeping the boundary marks of their holding in good repair.
* **Construction Orders:** Survey Officers can issue written notices (posted at the village *Chavadi*) requiring landholders to construct or repair specified boundary marks within a given timeframe.
* **Default Procedure:** If a holder fails to comply, the Survey Officer will construct or repair the marks directly and recover the expenses as an assessment on the landholder or interested parties.
* **Administrative Oversight:** Upon completion of a survey settlement, the **Deputy Commissioner** assumes overall charge of the boundary marks. Village Accountants and Panchayat officers are under a statutory duty to prevent the destruction, removal, or alteration of these marks.

---

## 4. Enforcement and Penalties

* **Summary Eviction:** If a boundary settlement reveals that a landholder is wrongfully occupying land outside their defined holding, the **Tahsildar** may summarily evict them.
* **Penalties for Injury or Tampering:** Anyone who willfully erases, removes, injures, or constructs an unauthorized boundary mark is liable to a fine of up to **₹50 per mark** following a summary inquiry. A portion of this fine may be awarded to an informer.
* **Local Authority Obligation:** Local authorities (such as Gram Panchayats) must actively prevent the destruction or tampering of boundary marks within their jurisdiction and report violations to the prescribed revenue officer.

---

## 5. Civil Court Jurisdiction Bar

Under **Chapter VI** of the Act, Civil Courts are explicitly barred from exercising jurisdiction over claims regarding boundaries fixed under the Act or orders relating to boundary and survey marks.
