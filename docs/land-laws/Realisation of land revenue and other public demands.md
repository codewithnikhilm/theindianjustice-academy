

Chapter XIV establishes the legal framework for enforcing the recovery of land revenue arrears and other statutory public dues. It outlines primary liability, sets down the State's statutory priority over all other creditors, and prescribes progressive enforcement processes ranging from notices to property attachment.

> 💡 **Analogy:** > The system for the realization of land revenue is like a priority queue at a bank, where the State Government holds a "permanent first-in-line" ticket; regardless of how many other creditors (like mortgage holders or banks) are waiting, the State's demand must be satisfied first before anyone else can claim the remaining assets.

---

## 1. Statutory Liability & Priority of State Claims

* **Primary Liability:**
  * **Unalienated Land:** The **occupant** is primarily responsible for payment.
  * **Alienated Land:** The **superior holder** is responsible.
  * **Joint Holdings:** Joint occupants or holders are **jointly and severally liable** for the total revenue due.
* **Precedence of Claim:** The State Government’s claim for land revenue and recoverable moneys holds absolute priority over all other private debts, mortgages, court judgments, or decrees against the land or its owner.
* **Lien on Agricultural Crops:** For agricultural land, the land revenue for the current revenue year forms a preferential claim enforceable against the standing or harvested crops of the land.

---

## 2. The Recovery Process

Land revenue becomes due on the first day of the revenue year. If an installment remains unpaid past the prescribed due date, the landholder is legally designated a **defaulter**, and the sum becomes an **arrear**. 

Recovery follows these progressive statutory stages:

1. **Notice of Demand:** A formal written demand notice is issued and served upon the defaulter once an arrear occurs.
2. **Forfeiture of Holding:** The Tahsildar may declare an occupancy or alienated holding forfeited to the State Government.
   > **Statutory Restriction:** Forfeiture **cannot** be declared if the total outstanding arrears do not exceed **₹10,000**.
3. **Distraint and Sale of Moveable Property:** The defaulter's moveable property (including crops/produce) may be seized and sold at auction.
   * *Exemptions:* Necessary items exempted under the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 (e.g., basic tools, wearing apparel) or items set aside for religious use are protected from distraint.
4. **Attachment and Sale of Immoveable Property:** If moveable assets are insufficient to satisfy the debt, the Tahsildar may attach and sell the defaulter's immoveable property.
5. **Government Management:** In complex cases involving entire villages or estates, the Deputy Commissioner may temporarily take the village under direct State management to recover arrears directly from local rents, profits, and revenue.

---

## 3. Recovery of Other Public Demands (Section 190)

The Act extends these coercive recovery powers beyond basic land revenue. Under **Section 190**, several categories of "public demands" can be recovered *as if they were arrears of land revenue*:

* Rent, royalties, and statutory water rates due to the State.
* Cesses, fees, charges, and administrative fines due to the State for using government land, water, or land products.
* Moneys due under any grant, lease, or government contract that includes a specific recovery clause as land revenue.
* Any other financial sums declared by other specific statutes to be recoverable as arrears of land revenue.

---

## 4. Precautionary & Supplemental Measures

* **Preventing Removal of Crops:** The Tahsildar may prohibit the removal or harvesting of crops from land until the current year's revenue is fully paid or secured—even before the actual due date arrives. This includes posting official watchmen over the crop at the defaulter's expense.
* **Recovery from Sureties:** If a guarantor or surety steps in for a principal debtor whose dues are recoverable as land revenue, the surety is subject to the exact same recovery processes.
* **Inter-District Recovery:** If a defaulter possesses property in a district other than where the debt arose, the Deputy Commissioner of the originating district can send a certified account of arrears to the Deputy Commissioner of the second district to execute the recovery directly.
