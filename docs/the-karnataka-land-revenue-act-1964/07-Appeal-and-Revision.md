# Appeals and Revision under the Karnataka Land Revenue Act, 1964
# Chapter V
# Sections 49 to 59

The procedure for **Appeal and Revision** within the revenue administration is detailed in **Chapter V of the Karnataka Land Revenue Act, 1964**. This Chapter establishes the hierarchy of appellate authorities, limitations on filing appeals, powers of appellate authorities, and the authority for revision.

# I. Appeals

The Act distinguishes between two types of appeals:

1. **Appeals from Original Orders (First Appeal)**
2. **Second Appeals**

## 1. Appeals from Original Orders — First Appeal

An appeal lies from every original order passed under the Act, the rules made thereunder, or any order made under Section 54 of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908.

The appellate authority depends upon the rank of the officer who passed the original order.

| Officer Passing the Original Order | Appellate Authority — First Appeal |
|---|---|
| Revenue Officer subordinate to the Assistant Commissioner, whether or not invested or delegated with the powers of the Assistant Commissioner or Deputy Commissioner | Assistant Commissioner |
| Assistant Commissioner, whether or not invested with the powers of the Deputy Commissioner | Deputy Commissioner |
| Deputy Commissioner | Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal |
| Regional Commissioner | Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal |
| Survey Officer below the rank of Assistant Director of Land Records or Assistant Director for Settlement | Assistant Director of Land Records or Assistant Director for Settlement |
| Survey Officer of the rank of Assistant Director of Land Records or Assistant Director for Settlement | Joint Director of Land Records or Joint Director for Settlement |
| Joint Director of Land Records or Joint Director for Settlement | Director of Survey, Settlement and Land Records |
| Director of Survey, Settlement and Land Records | Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal |

## 2. Second Appeals

A second appeal lies against an order passed in a first appeal under Section 49.

The hierarchy for second appeals is as follows:

- If the first appeal order was passed by the **Assistant Commissioner**, the second appeal lies to the **Deputy Commissioner**.
- If the first appeal order was passed by the **Deputy Commissioner**, the second appeal lies to the **Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal**.
- If the first appeal order was passed by the **Assistant Director for Settlement or Assistant Director of Land Records**, the second appeal lies to the **Director of Survey, Settlement and Land Records**.
- If the first appeal order was passed by the **Joint Director of Land Records, Joint Director for Settlement, or Director of Survey, Settlement and Land Records**, the second appeal lies to the **Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal**.

### Finality of Second Appeal

An order passed on second appeal shall be **final**.

# II. Appellate Procedure and Powers

## 1. Limitation of Appeals

The Act prescribes different periods for filing first and second appeals.

### First Appeal

A first appeal must be filed within the prescribed period of **60 days** from the date of the order appealed against.

### Second Appeal

A second appeal must be filed within the prescribed period of **90 days** from the date of the order appealed against.

### Application of the Limitation Act

The provisions of **Sections 4, 5 and 12 of the Limitation Act, 1963** apply *mutatis mutandis* to appeals under the Act.

## 2. Documents Required for Appeal

Every petition of appeal must ordinarily be accompanied by a **certified copy of the order appealed against**.

However, the appellate authority may waive this requirement.

## 3. Powers of the Appellate Authority

The appellate authority may, for reasons recorded in writing:

- Annul the order appealed against;
- Reverse the order;
- Modify the order;
- Confirm the order;
- Direct the officer who passed the order, or a subordinate officer, to conduct further inquiry;
- Direct the taking of additional evidence on specified points;
- Conduct such inquiry itself; or
- Take additional evidence itself.

These powers enable the appellate authority to examine both the legality and correctness of the order under appeal.

## 4. Restrictions on Additional Evidence

Additional oral or documentary evidence should not ordinarily be directed to be taken in appeal.

However, additional evidence may be permitted where:

1. The Revenue Officer whose order is appealed against refused to admit evidence which ought to have been admitted;
2. The appellate authority requires a document or witness to be examined in order to enable it to pronounce its order; or
3. The appellate authority considers that the evidence or document should be admitted for any other substantial cause.

Where additional evidence is allowed, the appellate authority must **record the reasons** for its admission.

## 5. Stay of Execution of Orders

The Act provides for stay of execution of an order during the appeal process.

### Stay by the Original Revenue Officer

The Revenue Officer who passed the order, or the officer who succeeds him, may direct that the execution of the order be stayed before the expiry of the appeal period.

Such stay may be granted for the period necessary to enable the aggrieved person to file an appeal and obtain a stay order from the appellate authority.

### Stay by the Appellate Authority

The appellate authority may direct that execution of the order appealed against be stayed:

- For such period as it considers appropriate; or
- Until the decision of the appeal,

whichever is earlier.

The appellate authority may also cancel or vary a stay order if sufficient cause is shown.

No stay order may be passed except in accordance with the statutory provisions.

## 6. Errors, Omissions and Irregularities

No order passed by a Revenue Officer shall be reversed or altered in appeal or revision merely because of an error, omission or irregularity in:

- Summons;
- Notice;
- Proclamation;
- Warrant;
- Order; or
- Other proceedings.

Such an error, omission or irregularity must have **actually resulted in a failure of justice** before it can justify interference with the order.

# III. Revision

The power of revision allows superior authorities and the Tribunal to examine the records of proceedings conducted by subordinate authorities.

## 1. Authorities Empowered to Exercise Revision

The following authorities may call for and examine the record of any inquiry or proceeding of a subordinate officer under the Act or under Section 54 of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908:

- The **Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal**;
- Any Revenue Officer not inferior in rank to an **Assistant Commissioner**; and
- Any Survey Officer not inferior in rank to a **Deputy Director of Land Records** or an **Assistant Settlement Officer** in their respective departments.

The purpose of examining the record is to satisfy themselves regarding the **legality or propriety** of the proceedings.

## 2. Subordination for the Purpose of Revision

For the purposes of the revision power under Section 56(1):

- A **Special Deputy Commissioner** is deemed not to be subordinate to the Deputy Commissioner.
- **All Revenue Officers are deemed to be subordinate to the Tribunal.**

This establishes the Tribunal as the highest revisional authority within the revenue hierarchy.

## 3. Powers in Revision

After examining the record, if the Tribunal or the empowered officer considers that a decision, order or proceeding should be:

- Modified;
- Annulled; or
- Reversed;

the authority may pass such order as it considers appropriate.

### Opportunity of Hearing

No order shall be modified, annulled or reversed unless:

1. Notice has been served upon the interested parties; and
2. The interested parties have been given an opportunity of being heard.

This requirement ensures compliance with the principles of **natural justice**.

## 4. Limitation for Revision

An application for revision must be presented within **four months** from the date of the order.

### Bar Against Revision Where Appeal Has Been Filed

No application for revision shall be entertained against an order in respect of which an appeal under Chapter V has already been preferred.

### Suo Motu Revision

A Revenue Officer or Survey Officer referred to in Section 56(1) may exercise **suo motu** power of revision in respect of an order against which no appeal has been preferred under Chapter V.

Such suo motu power may be exercised within **three years** from the date of the order sought to be revised.

### Exclusion of Time

While calculating the period of limitation, any period during which the revision proceeding is stayed by a court order or injunction shall be excluded.

# IV. Final Orders and the Tribunal

Where the Act declares that an order of a Revenue Officer shall be **final**, it means that no ordinary appeal lies against such order.

However, the **Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal** is competent to modify, annul or reverse such an expressly final order by exercising its revisional jurisdiction under Section 56.

Thus, statutory finality does not completely exclude the revisional jurisdiction of the Tribunal.

# V. Transitional Appeals and Amendments

The statutory provisions relating to appeals have undergone amendments to clarify the appellate hierarchy and centralise certain powers.

## 1. Appeals from Repealed Acts

In relation to orders or proceedings commenced and concluded under Acts repealed by the Karnataka Land Revenue Act, 1964, the appellate functions previously exercised by the Divisional Commissioners were entrusted to the **Mysore Revenue Appellate Tribunal**, which subsequently became the Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal.

## 2. Clarification Regarding Appeals from Revenue Officers

An amendment was made to Section 49 to clarify that an appeal against an order passed by a Revenue Officer subordinate to the Assistant Commissioner lies to the **Assistant Commissioner**, even if that Revenue Officer had been delegated or invested with the powers of the Assistant Commissioner or Deputy Commissioner.

This clarification prevents the delegated powers of an officer from altering the ordinary appellate hierarchy.

## 3. Appeals in Land Grant Cases

Appeals in land grant cases have undergone changes in the allocation of appellate and revisional jurisdiction.

Initially, such appellate powers were vested in the **Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal**. They were subsequently shifted to the Divisional Commissioners, with revision being vested in the Government.

Due to the high pendency of cases, appellate and revisional powers relating to land grant cases were subsequently transferred back to the **Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal in 1975**.

This strengthened the position of the Tribunal as a major appellate authority in revenue matters.

## 4. Abolition of Divisional Commissioners

Following proposals for the abolition of Divisional Commissioners, their appellate and revisional powers were proposed to be entrusted to the Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal.

# VI. Difference Between Appeal and Revision

The distinction between appeal and revision is important in understanding the revenue adjudicatory system.

| Basis | Appeal | Revision |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Statutory right of appeal | Supervisory/revisional jurisdiction |
| Purpose | Re-examination of the order by an appellate authority | Examination of legality or propriety of proceedings |
| Authority | Determined by the appellate hierarchy | Superior Revenue Officers, Survey Officers and Tribunal |
| Scope | Generally broader appellate examination | Primarily concerned with legality or propriety |
| Limitation | First Appeal — 60 days; Second Appeal — 90 days | Application — 4 months; Suo motu revision — up to 3 years in the specified circumstances |
| Notice | Parties are dealt with according to appellate procedure | Notice and opportunity of hearing are required before adverse modification, annulment or reversal |
| Appeal already filed | Appeal is the prescribed remedy | Revision generally cannot be invoked where an appeal has already been preferred |
| Final orders | Subject to the applicable appellate hierarchy | Tribunal may revise certain orders expressly declared final |

# VII. Revenue Appellate Hierarchy

The appellate structure can be broadly understood as follows:

### Revenue Officers Below Assistant Commissioner

**Revenue Officer → Assistant Commissioner → Deputy Commissioner → Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal**

### Assistant Commissioner

**Assistant Commissioner → Deputy Commissioner → Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal**

### Deputy Commissioner

**Deputy Commissioner → Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal**

### Regional Commissioner

**Regional Commissioner → Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal**

### Survey and Settlement Authorities

**Survey Officer → Assistant Director → Joint Director → Director of Survey, Settlement and Land Records → Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal**

# VIII. Conclusion

Chapter V of the **Karnataka Land Revenue Act, 1964** establishes a structured system of appeals and revision within the revenue administration of Karnataka.

The Act provides a clear appellate hierarchy, beginning with the Assistant Commissioner and Deputy Commissioner in appropriate cases and ultimately reaching the **Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal**.

It also provides safeguards relating to limitation, certified copies, additional evidence, stay of execution and procedural irregularities.

The revisional jurisdiction under Section 56 enables superior authorities and the Tribunal to examine the **legality and propriety** of proceedings conducted by subordinate authorities. The requirement of notice and an opportunity of hearing protects the principles of natural justice.

Consequently, the system of appeal and revision under the Karnataka Land Revenue Act provides multiple levels of scrutiny while maintaining a defined hierarchy of revenue authorities and specialised adjudication through the Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal.
