The Constitution and Powers of the Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal (KRAT) are set forth in Chapter IV of The Karnataka Land Revenue Act, 1964.
Constitution of the Tribunal
The State Government is responsible for constituting the Appellate Tribunal, known as the Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal, through notification. The Mysore Revenue Appellate Tribunal, constituted under the Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal Act, 1957, continued to function as the Tribunal for the purposes of the 1964 Act until a new Tribunal was duly constituted under Section 40.

The Tribunal consists of the following members, appointed by the State Government:
• Chairman: An officer of the rank of Regional Commissioner.
• Members: Five members, of whom:
    ◦ Three must be District Judges.
    ◦ The others (two members) must be officers having experience in administration of revenue matters not below the rank of a Deputy Commissioner.
The State Government has the authority to appoint additional members if necessary, due to an increase in business, arrears of work, or other reasons. These additional members must possess the qualifications specified for the members (District Judges or officers with revenue administration experience).
Conduct of Business and Sittings

1. Benches and Composition
• The Tribunal's powers concerning appeals, revisions, and other proceedings are exercised by a Bench of two members.
• Every such Bench must include a District Judge and an officer having experience in administration of revenue matters.
• The Chairman can constitute a full Bench consisting of not less than three members for hearing any appeal, revision, reference, or other proceeding, and must do so if a two-member Bench so thinks fit.

2. Single Member Powers
Notwithstanding the two-member requirement, a single member of the Tribunal may exercise powers in the following matters:
• Admission of an appeal or revision petition.
• Admission of an appeal or revision petition presented after the expiry of the period allowed by law.
• Stay orders pending the disposal of an appeal, revision, reference, or other proceeding.
• Any matter of an interlocutory character in appeals, revisions, references, or other proceedings.
Note on Single Member Orders: Any person aggrieved by an order rejecting an appeal or petition made by a single member may apply for revision, and that matter must be heard and disposed of by a Bench of two members.

3. Decision Making
• When a Bench consists of two or more members, the decision is made in accordance with the opinion of those members, or the majority opinion.
• If a two-member Bench differs in opinion on a material point, they must state the point, and the case shall then be heard upon that point only by another member of the Tribunal. The point is then decided according to the opinion of the majority of the members, including those who first heard it.

4. Location
The headquarters of the Tribunal is at Bangalore. However, Benches of the Tribunal may, subject to prescribed conditions, hold sittings at other places specified by the Chairman.

Powers of the Tribunal
The Tribunal exercises extensive appellate and revisional powers:

1. General Powers
The Tribunal exercises powers of appeal, reference, or revision vested in it by the Karnataka Land Revenue Act or any other law in force.

2. Transferred Powers
All appellate and revisional powers previously vested in or exercisable by the Mysore Revenue Appellate Tribunal constituted under the Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal Act, 1957, before the commencement of the 1964 Act, stand transferred to and are exercisable by the Tribunal constituted under Section 40. Any reference in any law to the Mysore Board of Revenue, the Mysore Revenue Appellate Tribunal, or the Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal is construed as a reference to the Tribunal constituted under Section 40.
The State Government may also confer upon or entrust the Tribunal with any appellate or revisional power or function assigned to the State Government or other authority/officer under any law currently in force. The Tribunal is competent to exercise these conferred powers.

3. Appellate Authority
The Tribunal is the appellate authority for:
• Original Orders:
    ◦ Orders passed by the Deputy Commissioner.
    ◦ Orders passed by the Regional Commissioner.
    ◦ Orders passed by the Director of Survey, Settlement and Land Records.
• Second Appeals:
    ◦ Orders passed by the Deputy Commissioner in a first appeal.
    ◦ Orders passed by the Joint Director of Land Records or Joint Director for Settlement, or by the Director of Survey, Settlement and Land Records in a first appeal.
The Tribunal has also taken on appellate and revisional powers concerning land grant cases, which had previously shifted between the Tribunal and the Divisional Commissioners.

4. Revisional Authority
The Tribunal has the power to call for and examine the record of any inquiry or proceedings of any subordinate officer under the Act or under Section 54 of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, to satisfy itself regarding the legality or propriety of the proceedings. For the purposes of revision, all Revenue Officers are deemed to be subordinate to the Tribunal.
The Tribunal is the only body competent to modify, annul, or reverse any order that is expressly declared final under the Act, using its powers of revision under Section 56.
If the Tribunal finds that a decision, order, or proceeding should be modified, annulled, or reversed, it may pass such orders as deemed fit, provided that interested parties are served notice and given an opportunity to be heard.

5. Powers of Review
The Tribunal may, either on its own motion or upon application by an affected party, review any order it has passed and issue necessary corresponding orders. An application for review by a party is only entertained if the Tribunal is satisfied that:
• There has been the discovery of new and important matter or evidence that, despite due diligence, was not known to the party or could not be produced when the order was passed; OR
• There is a mistake or error apparent on the face of the record; OR
• There is any other sufficient reason.
An application for review must be made within ninety days from the date of the order.

6. Judicial and Regulatory Powers
• Judicial Status: Any proceeding before the Tribunal is deemed a judicial proceeding for the purposes of specific sections of the Indian Penal Code, and the Tribunal is considered a Civil Court for the purposes of Chapter XXXV of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1898.
• General Directions: The Tribunal may call for returns from subordinate authorities and issue general directions and prescribe forms for regulating the practice and proceedings of such authorities, provided they are consistent with existing law.
• Regulations: The Tribunal may make regulations consistent with the Act and rules for generally regulating its practice and procedure, and the disposal of its business. This includes regulations concerning the time limit for appeals or applications when no express provision exists in the relevant enactment, and concerning costs. These regulations require previous publication and the sanction of the State Government.
Finality of Orders
Notwithstanding any law, every decision of the Tribunal is final and cannot be called into question in any Court, subject only to the Tribunal's own power of review under Section 44.

The Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal acts as the apex court for revenue matters within the State, akin to the Supreme Court for land administration issues. Its composition ensures a blend of judicial expertise (District Judges) and administrative experience (Revenue Officers), enabling it to provide final and reasoned decisions on appeals and revisions throughout the revenue hierarchy.
