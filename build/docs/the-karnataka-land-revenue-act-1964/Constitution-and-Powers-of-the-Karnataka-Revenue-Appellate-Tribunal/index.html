Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal (KRAT)

Chapter IV

Sections 40 to 48

The Constitution and Powers of the Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal (KRAT) are set forth in Chapter IV of the Karnataka Land Revenue Act, 1964.

1. Constitution of the Tribunal​

The State Government is responsible for constituting the Appellate Tribunal, known as the Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal, through notification.

The Mysore Revenue Appellate Tribunal, constituted under the Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal Act, 1957, continued to function as the Tribunal for the purposes of the 1964 Act until a new Tribunal was duly constituted under Section 40.

Composition of the Tribunal​

The Tribunal consists of the following members appointed by the State Government:

  • Chairman: An officer of the rank of Regional Commissioner.
  • Members: Five members, of whom:
    • Three must be District Judges.
    • Two must be officers having experience in the administration of revenue matters and not below the rank of a Deputy Commissioner.

The State Government may appoint additional members if necessary due to:

  • Increase in the business of the Tribunal;
  • Arrears of work; or
  • Any other reason considered necessary.

Such additional members must possess the qualifications prescribed for the regular members.

2. Conduct of Business and Sittings​

2.1 Benches and Composition​

The Tribunal's powers concerning appeals, revisions and other proceedings are exercised by a Bench of two members.

Every such Bench must include:

  • A District Judge; and
  • An officer having experience in the administration of revenue matters.

The Chairman may constitute a Full Bench consisting of not less than three members for hearing any appeal, revision, reference or other proceeding.

The Chairman must constitute such a Full Bench if a two-member Bench considers it necessary.

2.2 Single Member Powers​

Notwithstanding the requirement of a two-member Bench, a single member of the Tribunal may exercise powers in the following matters:

  • Admission of an appeal or revision petition;
  • Admission of an appeal or revision petition presented after the expiry of the prescribed period;
  • Granting stay orders pending disposal of an appeal, revision, reference or other proceeding; and
  • Any interlocutory matter arising in appeals, revisions, references or other proceedings.

2.3 Revision Against Single Member Orders​

Any person aggrieved by an order of a single member rejecting an appeal or petition may apply for revision.

Such revision must be heard and disposed of by a Bench of two members.

2.4 Decision Making​

When a Bench consists of two or more members, the decision is made according to:

  • The opinion of the members; or
  • The opinion of the majority where there is a difference of opinion.

If a two-member Bench differs on a material point, the members must state the point on which they differ.

The case is then heard upon that point only by another member of the Tribunal.

The disputed point is decided according to the opinion of the majority of the members, including the members who originally heard the case.

2.5 Location of the Tribunal​

The headquarters of the Tribunal is situated at Bangalore.

However, Benches of the Tribunal may, subject to the prescribed conditions, hold sittings at other places specified by the Chairman.

3. Powers of the Tribunal

The Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal exercises extensive appellate, revisional and other statutory powers.

3.1 General Powers​

The Tribunal exercises the powers of:

  • Appeal;
  • Reference; and
  • Revision

vested in it under the Karnataka Land Revenue Act or any other law in force.

3.2 Transferred Powers​

The appellate and revisional powers previously vested in or exercisable by the Mysore Revenue Appellate Tribunal under the Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal Act, 1957, before the commencement of the Karnataka Land Revenue Act, 1964, were transferred to and became exercisable by the Tribunal constituted under Section 40.

References in any law to:

  • Mysore Board of Revenue;
  • Mysore Revenue Appellate Tribunal; or
  • Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal;

are construed as references to the Tribunal constituted under Section 40.

The State Government may also confer upon or entrust the Tribunal with any appellate or revisional power or function assigned to the State Government or another authority or officer under any law in force.

The Tribunal is competent to exercise such powers when conferred upon it.

4. Appellate Authority​

The Tribunal functions as an appellate authority in respect of specified orders passed by revenue authorities.

4.1 Appeals Against Original Orders​

The Tribunal hears appeals against orders passed by:

  • Deputy Commissioner;
  • Regional Commissioner; and
  • Director of Survey, Settlement and Land Records.

4.2 Second Appeals​

The Tribunal also hears second appeals against certain orders passed in first appeals, including:

  • Orders passed by the Deputy Commissioner in a first appeal;
  • Orders passed by the Joint Director of Land Records or Joint Director for Settlement in a first appeal; and
  • Orders passed by the Director of Survey, Settlement and Land Records in a first appeal.

The Tribunal has also exercised appellate and revisional powers concerning land grant cases, which have historically shifted between the Tribunal and the Divisional Commissioners.

5. Revisional Authority

The Tribunal has the power to call for and examine the record of any inquiry or proceeding of a subordinate officer under the Act or under Section 54 of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908.

The purpose of exercising this power is to satisfy itself regarding the legality or propriety of the proceedings.

For the purposes of revision, all Revenue Officers are deemed to be subordinate to the Tribunal.

5.1 Orders Declared Final​

The Tribunal is the competent authority to modify, annul or reverse an order that is expressly declared final under the Act by exercising its revisional powers under Section 56.

If the Tribunal finds that a decision, order or proceeding should be:

  • Modified;
  • Annulled; or
  • Reversed;

it may pass such orders as it considers appropriate.

However, interested parties must be served notice and given an opportunity of being heard.

6. Powers of Review

The Tribunal may review an order passed by it:

  • On its own motion; or
  • On an application made by an affected party.

The Tribunal may issue any corresponding orders considered necessary after review.

6.1 Grounds for Review​

An application for review by an affected party may be entertained where the Tribunal is satisfied that:

  1. There has been the discovery of new and important matter or evidence which, despite due diligence, was not within the knowledge of the applicant or could not be produced when the order was passed; or
  2. There is a mistake or error apparent on the face of the record; or
  3. There is any other sufficient reason.

6.2 Limitation for Review​

An application for review must be made within 90 days from the date of the order.

7. Judicial and Regulatory Powers

The Tribunal possesses certain judicial and regulatory powers in addition to its appellate and revisional jurisdiction.

7.1 Judicial Status​

Proceedings before the Tribunal are deemed to be judicial proceedings for the purposes of the relevant provisions of the Indian Penal Code.

The Tribunal is also deemed to be a Civil Court for the purposes of Chapter XXXV of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1898.

7.2 Power to Call for Returns and Issue Directions​

The Tribunal may:

  • Call for returns from subordinate authorities;
  • Issue general directions to subordinate authorities; and
  • Prescribe forms for regulating the practice and proceedings of such authorities.

Such directions and forms must be consistent with the existing law.

7.3 Power to Make Regulations​

The Tribunal may make regulations consistent with the Karnataka Land Revenue Act and the rules made thereunder for regulating:

  • Its practice and procedure;
  • Disposal of its business;
  • Time limits for appeals or applications where no express provision exists in the relevant enactment; and
  • Costs.

Such regulations require:

  • Previous publication; and
  • Sanction of the State Government.

8. Finality of Orders

Subject to the Tribunal's own power of review under Section 44, every decision of the Tribunal is stated to be final and cannot be called into question in any Court.

The finality provision gives the Tribunal a significant position within the revenue adjudicatory structure of Karnataka.

9. Overall Role of the Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal

The Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal occupies a significant position in the revenue administration and adjudicatory system of Karnataka.

Its structure combines:

  • Judicial expertise, through the participation of District Judges; and
  • Revenue administration expertise, through officers experienced in revenue matters.

The Tribunal therefore provides a specialised forum for resolving disputes arising under revenue laws.

10. Conclusion​

The Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal is a specialised appellate and revisional body established under the Karnataka Land Revenue Act, 1964.

Its jurisdiction extends beyond ordinary appeals. It possesses powers of:

  • Appeal;
  • Revision;
  • Review;
  • Reference;
  • Regulation of procedure; and
  • Issuing directions to subordinate revenue authorities.

The Tribunal's composition of judicial members and experienced revenue administrators is intended to combine legal expertise with practical knowledge of revenue administration.

Its powers of revision and review further enable it to correct errors and ensure the legality and propriety of proceedings conducted by subordinate revenue authorities.

Thus, the Karnataka Revenue Appellate Tribunal plays an important role as a specialised revenue appellate and revisional authority in Karnataka.
